Swarm Street

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 19 November 2026

Swarm Street is a game made by an independent developer based in the Netherlands. This page explains what the game does with your information. The short answer: it does not collect any.

The short version

  • The game does not collect, send or share any personal information.
  • There are no accounts and no sign-in.
  • There are no adverts in the game.
  • There is nothing to buy in the game.
  • There are no analytics or tracking tools in the game.
  • The game works without an internet connection and does not contact any server.

What is stored, and where

The game saves your progress (the nights you have won, your stars and bolts, your workshop upgrades and your settings, including the language you chose) in files on your own device. Those files never leave your device through the game. We cannot see them.

NEW GAME in the game's settings starts again from the first night. If you delete the game, its saved progress is deleted with it. If your device makes its own backups (for example to your Apple or Google account), a backup may include the game's save and may bring it back when you restore the device; those backups are run by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies, not by us.

Reminders

The game can show reminders such as “Your post has arrived.” They are off unless you turn them on, and you can turn them off again in the game's settings or in your device's settings at any time. The reminders are planned on your own device. Nothing is sent to us or to anyone else to show them.

What we do not do

  • We do not ask for your name, age, email address, location, contacts, photos, microphone or camera.
  • We do not use advertising identifiers and we do not track you across other apps or websites.
  • We do not sell or share data with anyone, because we have none.

Other companies

The game contains no advertising, analytics or social-network software from other companies.

The store you download the game from (Apple's App Store or Google Play) and the maker of your device have their own privacy policies. They may collect information such as downloads and, if you have agreed to share them in your device settings, crash or usage reports. They may show us totals (for example, how many people downloaded the game) that do not identify you.

If you test an early version through Apple's TestFlight or a Google Play test, Apple or Google shares with us the email address you joined with and any feedback or screenshots you choose to send. We use these only to fix the game.

Children

The game can be played by children and by adults. Because the game does not collect personal information from anyone, it does not collect it from children. If we ever add anything that changes this, we will update this page first and, where the law requires it, ask a parent or guardian.

If you write to us

If you email us for help, we receive your email address and whatever you write. We use it only to answer you and we do not add it to any mailing list. We keep the conversation only as long as it is needed to help you, and you can ask us to delete it at any time.

Your rights

Under European privacy law (the GDPR) you may ask what personal data is held about you, and ask for it to be corrected or deleted. From the game itself we hold nothing. If you have written to us, write to the address below and we will show you, correct or delete those messages. You may also complain to the data protection authority in your country; in the Netherlands that is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens.

Changes

If the game changes in a way that affects your privacy (for example, if optional video adverts or purchases are added in a later version), we will change this page before that version is released and change the date at the top.
